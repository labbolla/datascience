# Statistical Analysis of Musculoskeletal Disorders in Musicians

Academic research project focused on the relationship between **physical activity, gender, musical instrument type and musculoskeletal disability in musicians**.

The study combines survey-based data collection, stratified sampling, standardized clinical questionnaires and inferential statistical analysis using a **2×2×2 factorial research design**.

## Original Thesis

**Il ruolo dell'attività motoria e sportiva nelle problematiche muscolo-scheletriche del musicista**

## Research Objective

Musicians are frequently exposed to repetitive movements, prolonged postures and biomechanical demands associated with playing musical instruments.

The research investigates whether regular physical or sports activity is associated with lower levels of musculoskeletal disability among musicians.

The study also examines whether disability scores differ according to:

- gender;
- physical activity status;
- musical instrument family.

Three anatomical areas were analyzed:

- lower back;
- upper limbs;
- neck.

## Research Hypotheses

Two main hypotheses were investigated.

### Hypothesis 1

Musicians who regularly practice physical or sports activity present lower levels of disability in the:

- neck;
- lower back;
- upper limbs

compared with musicians who do not regularly practice physical activity.

### Hypothesis 2

Female musicians present higher levels of musculoskeletal disability than male musicians.

## Data Collection

Data were collected through an **online survey** distributed among students and musicians associated with Italian conservatories.

Participants included musicians from institutions and communities connected with cities such as:

- Bari
- Foggia
- Palermo
- Milan
- Turin
- Naples
- Lecce

The questionnaire was administered voluntarily and anonymously.

Google Forms was used for online data collection.

## Sample

The initial survey collected responses from:

```text
203 participants
```

A stratified sampling procedure was subsequently applied.

The final balanced analytical sample contained:

```text
79 musicians
```

divided into:

| Group | Participants |
|---|---:|
| Physically active musicians | 40 |
| Non-active musicians | 39 |

The physically active group included:

```text
20 men
20 women
```

The non-active group included:

```text
20 men
19 women
```

Participants were further classified according to whether they played:

- string instruments;
- non-string instruments.

### Sample Characteristics

The overall sample had approximately:

| Variable | Mean ± SD |
|---|---:|
| Age | 24.58 ± 7.00 years |
| Weight | 68.44 ± 15.07 kg |
| Height | 171.68 ± 9.54 cm |

## Research Design

The research uses a **crossed factorial design with three fixed, non-manipulated factors**.

The factorial structure is:

```text
2 × 2 × 2
```

### Independent Variables

#### Physical Activity

```text
Active
Non-active
```

#### Gender

```text
Male
Female
```

#### Instrument Family

```text
String instruments
Non-string instruments
```

### Dependent Variables

Three standardized disability scores were analyzed:

```text
Oswestry Low Back Pain Disability Score
QuickDASH Score
Neck Disability Index Score
```

This design makes it possible to analyze both **main effects and interactions** between the three independent variables.

## Measurement Instruments

Three standardized self-administered questionnaires were used.

### Oswestry Low Back Pain Disability Questionnaire

The Oswestry questionnaire measures disability associated with lower-back problems.

It evaluates limitations in activities such as:

- personal care;
- walking;
- sitting;
- standing;
- sleeping;
- travelling;
- daily activities.

Higher scores indicate greater disability.

### QuickDASH

QuickDASH is a shortened version of the Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand questionnaire.

It measures symptoms and functional limitations affecting the upper limbs.

The questionnaire produces a score from:

```text
0 to 100
```

where higher values indicate greater disability.

### Neck Disability Index

The Neck Disability Index measures disability associated with neck pain and related functional limitations.

Higher scores correspond to greater disability.

## Statistical Analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using:

```text
JMP 10.0.1 — SAS Institute
```

Graphs were also produced using:

```text
Microsoft Excel 2013
```

The analysis included several statistical procedures.

## Descriptive Statistics

Continuous variables were summarized using:

```text
Mean ± Standard Deviation
```

Descriptive statistics were calculated for the different combinations of:

- physical activity;
- gender;
- instrument family.

## Student's t-test

Two-sided Student's t-tests were used to compare age and anthropometric characteristics between groups.

No statistically significant age differences were detected between active and non-active participants.

A significant difference was observed for anthropometric characteristics among male participants.

## Normality Assessment

Sample distributions were evaluated using:

**Shapiro-Wilk test**

Normality was assessed before applying inferential statistical procedures.

## Homogeneity of Variance

The equality of variances between groups was evaluated using:

**Levene's test**

When assumptions regarding equal variances or sample size were problematic, alternative statistical approaches were considered.

## Factorial ANOVA

The main inferential analysis used a:

**2×2×2 Factorial ANOVA**

The three factors were:

```text
Physical Activity × Gender × Instrument Family
```

The model evaluated:

- main effects;
- two-factor interactions;
- three-factor interactions.

The analysis was performed separately for each disability questionnaire.

## Robust & Non-Parametric Procedures

Additional procedures were used when the assumptions required by conventional ANOVA were potentially violated.

These included:

- **Welch ANOVA**
- **t-test for unequal variances**
- **Kruskal-Wallis test**

Kruskal-Wallis was used as a non-parametric confirmation procedure for small or non-normally distributed groups.

## Effect Size

The strength of association between predictors and disability scores was evaluated using:

**Adjusted R²**

The interpretation used in the study was:

```text
R² < 0.10       → negligible effect
R² = 0.10–0.30  → small effect
R² = 0.30–0.50  → moderate effect
R² > 0.50       → large effect
```

Statistical significance was defined as:

```text
p < 0.05
```

## Main Results

### Lower Back Disability — Oswestry

A statistically significant difference was found according to **gender**:

```text
F = 15.41
p = 0.0002
Adjusted R² = 0.16
```

Female participants showed higher lower-back disability scores than male participants.

No statistically significant main effect of physical activity was detected for this outcome.

## Upper-Limb Disability — QuickDASH

QuickDASH produced two statistically significant main effects.

### Physical Activity

```text
F = 4.05
p = 0.0479
Adjusted R² = 0.03
```

Musicians who practiced physical or sports activity showed lower upper-limb disability scores overall than non-active musicians.

### Gender

```text
F = 22.28
p < 0.0001
Adjusted R² = 0.20
```

Female musicians showed higher upper-limb disability scores than male musicians.

The gender association was substantially stronger than the association with physical activity.

## Neck Disability Index

A statistically significant difference was also detected according to gender:

```text
F = 8.62
p = 0.0045
Adjusted R² = 0.10
```

Female musicians showed higher neck disability scores than male musicians.

Physical activity did not produce a statistically significant main effect for neck disability.

## Summary of Significant Findings

| Outcome | Significant Factor | p-value | Adjusted R² |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Lower-back disability | Gender | 0.0002 | 0.16 |
| Upper-limb disability | Physical activity | 0.0479 | 0.03 |
| Upper-limb disability | Gender | < 0.0001 | 0.20 |
| Neck disability | Gender | 0.0045 | 0.10 |

## Interpretation

The first research hypothesis was **partially supported**.

Regular physical or sports activity was associated with significantly lower disability scores for the **upper limbs**, but statistically significant differences were not detected for the neck or lower back.

The second hypothesis was supported more consistently.

Female musicians showed significantly higher disability scores for:

- lower back;
- upper limbs;
- neck.

The results also suggested some differences between string and non-string musicians, although these differences were not statistically significant in the factorial analysis.

## Study Limitations

Several limitations were identified in the original research.

The final analytical sample was relatively small and had a young average age.

Data were collected through an online self-administered questionnaire, meaning that the researcher could not directly verify:

- participant attention during questionnaire completion;
- complete understanding of instructions;
- reporting accuracy.

Physical activity was also considered in general terms rather than according to a standardized preventive training protocol.

Therefore, the results support statistical associations within the analyzed sample but should not be interpreted as definitive causal evidence.

## Conclusions

The study found evidence that regular physical activity is associated with **lower upper-limb disability among musicians**, although similar statistically significant differences were not identified for the neck or lower back.

Gender showed a stronger and more consistent statistical association with musculoskeletal disability, with female musicians presenting higher disability scores across all three evaluated anatomical regions.

The research illustrates the use of a complete statistical workflow applied to real survey data, combining **sampling, hypothesis testing, factorial ANOVA, assumption checking, effect-size estimation and non-parametric validation techniques**.
